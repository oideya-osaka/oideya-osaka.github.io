A kominka home,

Oideya is a whole-house rental in a 1931 kominka, renovated with a modern touch.
Spend a few days living, not just visiting, in Kanzakigawa.

CONCEPT

Japanese calm,
meets everyday freedom.

Built in 1931, this kominka was renovated from the ground up together with a local carpenter. Tatami mats, shoji screens and exposed beams meet cobalt-blue furniture and art prints — a space that feels both nostalgic and new.

More than sightseeing, Oideya is about taking in the everyday air of Kanzakigawa itself — that's the stay we've set out to make.

ACCESS

3 minutes to the station,
6 to Umeda.

It's a 3-minute walk to Hankyu Kanzakigawa Station, and just 6 minutes by train to Umeda — central Osaka is right on your doorstep. One Hankyu line takes you to Kobe-Sannomiya in about 30 minutes, and to Kyoto-Kawaramachi (via Juso) in about 50 — easy day trips across Kansai. From Kansai International Airport, it's roughly 60–70 minutes via the JR/Nankai and Hankyu lines.

ROOMS

Old and new,
living side by side.

The ground floor of this two-storey house is one open living-dining-kitchen space, with bedrooms upstairs. Three double beds plus two futons sleep up to 8. For winter, we've added a kotatsu, supplemental heaters, and insulating curtains and noren to keep the cold out.

THE AREA

Beyond the sights:
everyday life in Kanzakigawa.

Hankyu Oasis

5 min walk. A bright, open supermarket right by Kanzakigawa Station. Fresh vegetables, fish and meat, plus a wine corner that's easy to get drawn into. Look for the lit-up storefront at night.

Shunkashuto Izakaya

2 min walk. A seafood izakaya built around what's in season — thick-cut sashimi, firefly squid in vinegar-miso, and seared wagyu that pairs perfectly with warm sake. A great stop between sightseeing.

Yoruca Cafe

4 min walk. A Western-style cafe known for its runny-egg, melted-cheese toast. Hearty breakfasts, and mobile ordering means you don't need to speak Japanese to order.

Mitsuya Shopping Street

Within 5 min walk. A row of old-fashioned shops that shows a side of Osaka the tourist spots don't. Take a walk and feel the neighborhood's everyday rhythm.

FAQ

Frequently Asked
Questions

How do I check in?

There's no staff on site — check-in is self-service with a door code. We'll send the code and directions a few days before your stay.

Can I smoke?

The entire building is non-smoking. As neighbors live close by, please also refrain from smoking around the property.

Is it okay if I don't speak Japanese?

We can message in English, Korean and Chinese. Most of our guests come from overseas.

What's the minimum and maximum number of guests?

You get the whole house, for up to 8 guests. Smaller groups are just as welcome.

Is there parking?

There's no dedicated parking, but paid coin parking is available nearby. Since the station is within walking distance, arriving by train is very convenient.

Can I bring a pet?

We're sorry, but pets are not allowed.

Is Wi-Fi available?

Yes, high-speed Wi-Fi is provided. Connection details are posted inside the house.

Can I watch TV?

There's no terrestrial broadcast TV. The large TV supports streaming services like Netflix and Amazon Prime Video, but you'll need to log in with your own account to watch.

What's the cancellation policy?

Bookings made through an OTA (Airbnb, Booking.com, Agoda) follow that platform's cancellation policy. For direct bookings, cancellations up to 3 days before check-in are free; after that, a one-night cancellation fee applies.

Are there supermarkets or convenience stores nearby?

Convenience stores and a supermarket are within a 5-minute walk. The local Mitsuya shopping street is right nearby too, for a taste of the neighborhood.
